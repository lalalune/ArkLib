# Four values per error-locator pencil, and three for subgroup-coset pencils

For the [single-hole problem](astra_mca_single_hole_locator-2026-09-05.md),
one two-dimensional constant-coefficient space of error locators contributes
at most **four distinct values** at the omitted point. On the production
subgroup, the subclass whose private locators are binomials contributes at
most **three**; an explicit construction attains three on that actual domain.

These results use actual divisors of the punctured domain polynomial. There
is also an exact realization converse: an admissible pencil of disjoint
private root sets can be made into genuine single-hole MCA witnesses.
An exhaustive computation over the actual production field excludes the
order-sixteen cubic pattern that could supply a large common power lift of
a four-member pencil.

The arguments are not Lean-formalized. Crucially, the complete set of valid
locators need not lie in one pencil. No bound on the number of such pencils,
universal production value bound, or over-budget construction is proved.

## Four values per pencil

Use integer `b>=3` and the parameters

```text
n=6b-2, N=n-1=6b-3, k=3b-1,
A=4b-1, e=N-A=2b-2.
```

Let `D=Omega\{a}`, and let v be a word on D. A valid locator is a monic
degree-e divisor Lambda of `P_D`, with associated polynomial f of degree
less than k agreeing with v outside the roots of Lambda. This permits
padded error supports, exactly as in the remainder formulation.
The associated f is unique because `A>=k`.

Fix a two-dimensional K-linear polynomial space, and select one valid
locator for each distinct value `f(a)` supplied by that space. If there is
at most one value there is nothing to prove. Otherwise two selected
locators span the space. Let G be their monic gcd and `g=degree G`.
Every pair of different projective members has this same gcd: a constant
invertible change of basis preserves the ideal generated by the pair.

Write the selected locators as `Lambda_i=G*U_i`. All private polynomials
`U_i` are monic of degree `s=e-g`, pairwise coprime, and coprime to G.
Their roots and those of G lie in D, so for m selected locators

```text
m*(e-g)+g <= N.
```

For two different selected values, `f_i-f_j` is a nonzero polynomial of
degree at most `k-1`. It vanishes outside the union of the two locator root
sets, hence at at least

```text
N-2e+g = 2b+1+g
```

nodes. The root bound gives `g<=b-3`. Therefore

```text
m <= floor((N-g)/(e-g))
  <= floor(5b/(b+1)).
```

This is at most four, and at `b=3` it is at most three. The ratio increases
with g, so the second inequality uses precisely `g<=b-3`.

Four values in one pencil would require

```text
b+1 <= s <= floor((4b-1)/3),
g=2b-2-s.
```

After removing G, they would give four pairwise coprime monic degree-s
divisors `U_i` lying in one affine polynomial pencil, with
`G*product_i U_i` dividing `P_D`.

## Exact realization from private divisors

The preceding divisor patterns are sufficient for constructing a received
word; they are not merely necessary incidence counts.

Suppose `m>=2`, the field has more than m elements, and

```text
U_i=C+c_i*B,       c_i pairwise distinct,
degree U_i=s,      U_i monic,
G*product_i U_i divides P_D,
degree G=g=e-s,    0<=g<=b-3.
```

The divisor condition makes all the displayed factors pairwise coprime and
squarefree. Set `R=P_D/(G*product_i U_i)`, label one member as 0, and define

```text
f_0=0,
f_i=(c_0-c_i)*R*product_{j not in {0,i}} U_j    for i!=0.
```

For every pair of different indices, direct substitution of
`U_i=C+c_i*B` gives

```text
f_i-f_j=(c_j-c_i)*R*product_{l not in {i,j}} U_l.
```

Each nonzero f_i has degree

```text
degree R+(m-2)*s = N-g-2s = N-2e+g <= k-1.
```

At a root of `U_j`, every `f_i` with `i!=j` has the same value, and `f_j`
has a different value. Define v to be that common value there. At a root
of R all f_i vanish, so put v equal to zero. At a root of G the f_i values
are all distinct; choose v outside those m values. This is possible by the
field-size assumption.

Then the exact error set of f_i is the root set of `G*U_i`. Each f_i has
exactly A punctured agreements. At a, all factors on the right side of the
difference identity are nonzero, so the values `f_i(a)` are pairwise
distinct. The single-hole equivalence therefore supplies m genuine MCA
scalars on the full domain at agreement `A+1=4b`.

For `m=2` the products excluding the two indices are empty products, equal
to one. Thus `f_1=(c_0-c_1)*R`; all degree, exact-error, and value-distinctness
claims remain valid. The checker includes this endpoint with a non-binomial
private pencil.

## The sharp binomial bound on the production subgroup

Now take `Omega=mu_n`, `n=2^30`, and `a=1`. Suppose the private factors have
the form

```text
U_i=X^s-c_i.
```

A squarefree degree-s binomial splitting entirely on `mu_n` has `s|n`:
ratios of its roots give all s-th roots of unity inside `mu_n`. Thus s is
a power of two. Two distinct decoded values already require

```text
b+1<=s<=e=2b-2.
```

At `b=178956971`, the only power of two in this interval is
`s=n/4=268435456`. There are four cosets of `mu_s` in `mu_n`, and the one
containing a is unavailable to a private locator. Consequently at most
three distinct values occur in this binomial class.

The bound is attained on the actual production domain. Choose the three
cosets not containing a as the private root sets. Put

```text
s=n/4=(3b-1)/2,
g=e-s=(b-3)/2=89478484.
```

Choose G from any g nodes of the a-coset other than a. The remaining factor
R consists of the b unused nodes of that coset. The realization construction
above gives three distinct values, with polynomial degree

```text
degree R+s = b+s = 447392427 < k=536870912.
```

This constructs three values, not an exhaustive production list. It does
not assert that other locators for the constructed word lie in this pencil.

## Excluding an order-sixteen cubic power-lift pattern

The checker uses the actual production prime

```text
P=365375409332725729550921208179070755120141565953
```

from the existing [prime certificate](../../ArkLib/Data/CodingTheory/ProximityGap/Frontier/_PrizeShapePrimeP30.lean).
It verifies the required root orders by modular exponentiation; it does not
rebuild the primality proof.

Over this field there is **no pencil containing four pairwise coprime monic
cubic divisors of the locator of `mu_16\{1}`**. The finite computation is
exhaustive: 455 cubic divisors, 50,050 unordered disjoint pairs, and 28 lines
containing at least three vertices from those pairs. The largest disjoint
family on any line has size three. One three-member witness, in exponents
of the chosen primitive sixteenth root, is

```text
{1,6,9}, {2,4,8}, {7,14,15}.
```

Here is the canonical-line algorithm. A monic cubic is represented by its
three remaining coefficients p in `K^3`. For distinct points p,q, let j be
the first nonzero coordinate of `p-q`; normalize the direction d so
`d_j=1`, and set the anchor to `p-p_j*d`. The pair `(d,anchor)` uniquely
identifies the affine line. Record the endpoints of every disjoint pair
under that key, then check the resulting vertex sets for disjoint families.
Any four-member disjoint pencil would have all six of its pairs recorded
under one key. Root counting permits at most five disjoint cubic members
on the fifteen available nodes, so the finite search covers every possible
family size.

The exclusion also holds in characteristic zero and over every prime field
`F_p` with

```text
p = 1 mod 16,     p > 72^8 = 722204136308736.
```

Here is the norm argument transferring the finite certificate. Fix a
primitive sixteenth root zeta in characteristic zero and work in
`K=Q(zeta)`, whose degree is eight. The three nonleading coefficients of a
monic cubic with roots among the powers of zeta are algebraic integers.
Under every complex embedding their absolute values are at most three,
by the elementary-symmetric coefficient formulas. A coordinate difference
between two such cubics therefore has absolute value at most six. Every
two-by-two minor Delta of coefficient-difference columns satisfies

```text
|sigma(Delta)| <= 2*6*6 = 72,
|Norm_K/Q(Delta)| <= 72^8.
```

Consider any four disjoint root-index triples from `{1,...,15}`. Their
coefficient points are not collinear over the production field, by the
exhaustive census. Thus at least one two-by-two minor of the three
difference columns relative to the first point reduces to a nonzero value
there. The corresponding Delta in `Z[zeta]` is nonzero. It remains nonzero
in characteristic zero, and its norm is a nonzero integer of absolute
value at most `72^8`. For a prime p as above, reduction of Delta at any
primitive sixteenth root in `F_p` cannot vanish: otherwise p would divide
that nonzero norm. Hence these four cubics remain noncollinear over every
such prime field. This proves the universal exclusion without a separate
enumeration of quadruples; the line census already covers every quadruple,
and the same norm bound applies to whichever minor certifies it.

This has a precise production implication. If the private polynomials of
a four-member production pencil all belonged to `K[X^ell]`, for
`ell=n/16`, their degree range would force `s/ell=3`. Their descended
polynomials would be a forbidden four-member cubic pencil on
`mu_16\{1}`. Larger power-of-two lifts have no integer private degree in
the required interval. Thus these common power lifts are excluded.

Equivalently, common invariance of the private root sets under `mu_ell`
is excluded for those orders. The equivalence uses monicity and nonzero
constant terms: root invariance gives
`U(zeta*X)=zeta^s*U(X)`; comparing constants gives `ell|s`, after which
coefficient comparison yields `U in K[X^ell]`.

The next case, a common `X^(n/32)` lift, would have private degree six or
seven on `mu_32\{1}`. **Those cases are not excluded here.** Neither are
general rational right factors or unstructured production private root sets.

## Relation to the two-generator Padé description

The standard two-generator description uses polynomial coefficients, not
two constants. Rational-interpolation decoding and its relation to the
Johnson radius are treated by
[Beelen, Hoholdt, Nielsen, and Wu](https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.0122).
Bounding each constant-coefficient locator pencil does not bound all the
polynomial combinations of the generators. The unresolved production task
is still to bound the value image of all actual divisor locators together.

Run `python3 scripts/probes/astra_mca_locator_pencils_check.py`. It checks
the construction on a length-sixteen subgroup over the actual prime, the
sharp four-member construction on a separate generic length-22 domain over
F101, the two-member endpoint, the cubic-pencil census, and the production
degree and norm-bound arithmetic. The infinite-prime transfer uses the
written norm proof, in addition to that finite census. All nine constructed
finite witnesses are also checked
against the original same-support scalar and no-joint rank conditions.
It does not enumerate the production domain.
